How it works

1. We find the company in business databases (Crustdata), using its website or name.

2. We gather evidence from its profile (staff, history, revenue estimate), its job ads, its website, website registration records, the news, and the US sanctions list.

3. We run automatic checks. Each asks a simple question like "Does it have the staff it claims?" and answers OK, Concern or Red flag.

4. We work out a risk level using fixed rules from the due-diligence checklist. The AI does not decide the verdict.

5. AI writes the plain-English summary, possible innocent explanations, and the questions to ask the supplier — using only the findings above.

Common questions

What do the verdicts mean?
✓ Looks legitimate — no real warning signs.
! Needs a human review — some warning signs; ask for documents.
✕ High risk — serious warning signs or a deal-breaker.
? Not enough data — we couldn't find enough to judge.

What is a deal-breaker?
A check so serious (e.g. being on a sanctions list) that the case must go to a senior person, whatever the score.

What does "Linked companies" show?
Anything this company shares with other companies we have checked — the same director, address, phone or tax ID. Many companies sharing one director or address is a classic sign of a shell network.

Is "couldn't check" bad?
No. Many genuine small businesses have little online presence. It only lowers how sure we are.

This tool shows warning signs to support your decision. It is not a legal finding.
